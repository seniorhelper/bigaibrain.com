Day one: we built an AI from scratch, and it's adorable

Today, October 12, 2026, Big AI Brain was born. It has 384 neurons, about 483,000 connections, and absolutely no idea what a rock is. We couldn't be prouder.

Why start dumb?

We could have bolted on one of the big AI engines and had a smooth-talking chatbot in an afternoon. We didn't, on purpose. Big AI Brain's growing neural network starts as pure random noise and learns only from what people choose to feed it. That means it babbles at first. It also means every bit of intelligence it ever shows was given to it by a real person who wanted to help. That's the whole point.

What it can do on day one

Dumb doesn't mean boring. Alongside the growing brain are hand-wired "lobes" that work from the first minute:

  • 171 clean jokes and 30 knock-knocks. It tells them, and it'll play along if you start one.
  • A math lobe that shows its work, plus a quiz that levels up as you get answers right.
  • 76 facts and 18 mini lessons, heavy on octopuses.
  • A memory lobe: when a human approves something you fed it, the brain can use it immediately and credits you by name.
  • An "under the hood" lobe that explains its own code with diagrams. Yes, it can teach you to code.
  • A five-layer safety lobe and a rewind button, because the internet is the internet.

What happens next

Every night at 3:15 am Denver time, the brain studies everything approved that day and takes the same test it takes every night. When the score drops low enough and enough meals have been approved, it evolves: Single Cell, Amoeba, Fish, Caveman, Toddler, Kid, Teen, Graduate. Each stage adds real neurons and a new look. We'll post a snapshot of every upgrade in the evolution log.

Want to be part of day one? Feed it something you know. Teach it what a rock is. Somebody has to.
